## Measurements

**Contrast** (`test/visual-backdrop-contrast.test.ts`). Nothing on the battle
stage is drawn on bare art: every word and glyph sits on an HP box, an opaque
panel, so no backdrop can move a text reading, and the test asserts that
first. What a backdrop can reach is the HP box's edge. The test reads it
against WCAG's 3:1 for components: 9 backdrops by 10 field states, two boxes
each, 180 readings. **Lowest 3.51**: the foe's box on badlands and on cave
under psychic terrain. The weakest pairs 5.0/2 named by eye (snow on summit,
sun and sand on badlands) all measure above that.

**Bytes.** The icons are 4,215 bytes as files. Under Vite's 4 KB inline limit
they are inlined into the one script, so the first load grows by 8,209 bytes
raw, **3,845 gzipped (+0.47%)**. CSS grows by 126 gzipped, and there are no
new requests: the 17 PNGs fetched are the backdrops, each when its screen
first shows. The repainted gym is 138 bytes smaller in the build.

## For the reviewer

- **The slot grids are the open question.** On most backdrops the outer
  three-option slots (16 to 24% and 76 to 84% of the width) land off the
  painted ground on some rows. Examples: water on the shore and the marsh,
  ice on the summit, walls in the cave and the ruins, stairs and lamp posts
  in the city, lava at the top left of the badlands. 5.0/4 read every node as
  standing in a clearing, but that was one seed's nodes, not every slot.
  Narrowing the grid is the obvious fix, and it breaks the decision row: the
  step being chosen from carries the whole card, up to about 106px wide, and
  three slots on today's grid sit about 107px apart at 390 and 103px at 375.
  **Options:**
  1. **Accept it.** The nodes are discs with a ring; they read as tokens on a
     map, not as figures standing in water. A known difference.
  2. **Narrow the grid per backdrop and let the current row's detail wrap**
     to two lines when three cards are offered. That costs height on the
     decision row, which the fit gate measures.
  3. **Repaint with a wider clearing.** The author's paintings, the
     author's call.

  **Recommended: 1 now**, and 3 if the paintings are ever redone, because 2
  trades a fact's legibility for scenery.
- **WebKit and a real iPhone are the reviewer's**, as at every 5.0 stage. The
  marks are CSS masks (`-webkit-mask` and `mask` both written); Chromium draws
  them crisp at 16 and 24, and Safari is the engine to look at.
- **The relics are a first pass by the session.** Any of them, or any icon,
  can be replaced by dropping a file over it.
