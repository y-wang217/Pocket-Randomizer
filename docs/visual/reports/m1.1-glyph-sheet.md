## The kills-it condition fired once, and the band pips were redrawn

M1.1: *"two glyphs in the same family are indistinguishable at 16px under any
simulation. Redraw before mounting."*

The first measurement put **`band-pip-on` against `band-pip-off` at 0.063**,
against a floor of 0.12. The cause is worth recording because it is a fact about
the shipped tree rather than about a drawing:

> `.band__pip` in `styles.css` is a box of `--bg-sunken` with a hairline;
> `[data-on="true"]` is the same box in `--text-dim`. **The two states differ in
> fill tone and in nothing else.** As shapes they are the same mark twice.

A tone difference is a real channel and it survives all three simulations, since
each preserves luminance. It is not enough on its own at 16px, against a
background whose own tone the density modes move. The redraw is **filled against
outlined**, which keeps the tone difference and adds a shape difference:
**0.063 → 0.262**.

Section 2 is unmoved by this and no amendment was needed: *"one pip per band,
filled to band"* says what a filled pip means and says nothing about how an
empty one is drawn.

**This is the item earning its tier.** The band strip ships today at half the
separation floor, and M1.1 found it because it measured before anything mounted
it, rather than after a surface depended on it.

| accuracy | 0.598 | target vs never-miss |

`pp` and `effectiveness` hold one glyph each and have no pair, so they do not
appear. 828 comparisons in total.

**The scores do not move under simulation**, and `test/glyphs.test.ts` asserts
that they do not. Every mark is monochrome `currentColor`, so a colour matrix
cannot change a shape score. That is not a weakness of the measurement, it is
the property section 2 asks for — *"glyph is primary, colour secondary"* — held
as a number.

## What the four pictures are actually for

The two families that carry colour in the game: type chips and status chips.
Both are rendered in the sheet using the app's own stylesheet, so the colours are
the ones that ship rather than ones invented for a report.

**Read `m1.1-glyphs-deuteranopia.png` beside `m1.1-glyphs-none.png`.** Under
deuteranopia the type chips collapse hard — GROUND, ELECTRIC and STELLAR land on
one yellow, FIGHTING and DRAGON on one tan — while the glyphs beside them stay
apart. That is section 2's colour-secondary rule doing its job, and it is the
argument for M2.1 putting the glyph *in* the chip rather than leaving it behind
the card as a watermark.

The status chips hold up better: the six are separated by their lettering first,
so the simulations cost them nothing a reader would notice.

## Decisions taken while building

1. **The sheet stores inner SVG markup, not a bare `d`.** That is the shape
   `typeIcons.ts` already stores, and it is what lets the eighteen type glyphs be
   referenced rather than restated. A glyph wanting two subpaths, a stroke or an
   even-odd rule says so in its own markup.
2. **`currentColor` throughout.** No glyph in the sheet carries colour. Type
   colour and status colour are chip properties, applied on top.
3. **The floor of 0.12 is a judgement and is written down as one.** It is
   roughly "an eighth of the box disagrees", which at 16px is two or three
   strokes. Every pair's real number is in the JSON so the floor can be argued
   with rather than trusted.
4. **The separation is held from a committed measurement, not re-measured under
   `npm test`.** Measuring means a build, a browser and 42 rasterisations, about
   a minute. The test checks that the committed measurement covers the current
   roster, so a glyph added or redrawn without re-measuring fails rather than
   inheriting someone else's number.

## The gate, and one flake worth naming

Green: **149 files, 1914 tests**, lint and typecheck clean, `contentHash`
unmoved at `d4e080`. No file under `src/data/` or `src/core/` changed, and the
one new module under `src/ui/` is imported by nothing that renders.

**`test/visual-move-cards.test.ts` failed once, on the full suite, and passes in
isolation.** It reported six of its seven surfaces, missing `result`. Nothing in
M1.1 is reachable from the app, so it is not this item's failure, and it is
recorded here rather than shrugged off because the cause is structural:

> The test drives a real run with `stepOnce` in a loop, separated by fixed
> `waitForTimeout(25)` and a step budget. The waits are wall-clock, not state,
> so on a machine saturated by the rest of the suite a step that has not
> finished rendering is counted as a step taken, and the walk can exhaust its
> budget before it reaches the last surface.

That is a wall-clock assertion of the kind
`docs/visual/reports/patch-battle-animation.md` already retired elsewhere in
this tree. **It is not M1.1's to fix** — the item touches no screen — but it is
the sort of thing that turns an unattended overnight run into a false red, so it
is named here for whoever picks up the next flaky gate.
