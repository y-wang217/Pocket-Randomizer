### D22. R9's precedence ranks seven outcomes; the vocabulary has fifteen kinds

**Blocks M4.1.**

R9: *"at most one flag appears on a target, by fixed precedence: no effect,
miss, super effective or not very effective, critical, status inflicted, berry
fired, stat stage changed."* Seven entries.

`FlagKind` carries seventeen. Take away `stab` and `contact`, which M4.1
deletes, and fifteen remain. R9 ranks eight of them (`immune`, `miss`, `super`,
`resisted`, `crit`, `status`, `berry`, and `boost`/`unboost` as one entry). It
says nothing about the other six: `priority`, `prevented`, `failed`, `ability`,
`volatile`, `field`.

Those six are not an oversight in the mapper. They are the abnormality classes
the battle-animation run measured over 699 battles
([`../reports/battle-anim-2-protocol-census.md`](../reports/battle-anim-2-protocol-census.md)),
and M4.1's own text says to **keep the seven measured kinds**. R9 predates them.

There is also a scoping question inside the same row. R9 binds *a hit on a
target*. `field` is about neither Pokemon; `priority` is about turn order;
`prevented` describes a turn in which no hit happened at all. A precedence that
ranks them against `crit` is ranking things that never compete.

**Options.**

1. **One flag per action, one precedence list over all fifteen.** Simplest to
   state, simplest to test, and the strip becomes at most two chips on a
   two-action turn plus residual. Requires amending R9's enumeration to name
   the fifteen.
2. **R9 binds hits only; the non-hit kinds are a second channel.** `priority`,
   `field`, `prevented` and `failed` are not outcomes on a target, so they
   render beside the one hit flag rather than competing with it. No rule
   changes; section 4's budget row needs a sentence saying what the second
   channel costs.
3. **One flag per turn.** The hardest cut, and the one that most nearly matches
   the strip's single line. It drops the second actor's outcome entirely on
   every two-action turn, which is C2 territory.

**Recommendation: 2**, with the fifteen-kind order still written down in data so
the ranking inside each channel is a table rather than an argument. It leaves
R9 exactly as written, it keeps `prevented` — the class that exists *because* a
flinched turn draws no damage and would otherwise be invisible — and the second
channel is bounded: at most one non-hit kind per side, taken in protocol order.

**What it costs, either way:** today the strip shows every flag of the group.
Under any of the three, facts leave the strip. `ui/abnormality.ts` assumes the
strip carries what the animation drops. The log sheet carries everything and is
one tap away, the panel carries status and stage independently, and an emptied
item slot carries the berry — that is the C2 re-encoding argument, and it should
be written into the item's report rather than assumed.

### D23. The budget is one *word* per hit; nine flag words are two or three

**Blocks M4.1 and M4.3.**

Section 4, Rev 2, the row D7 added: *"Flag strip (battle) | 1 per hit | The one
flag word R9 allows."* Section 4 counts words, excluding proper nouns and bare
numbers.

`FLAG_WORDS` and `flagWord()` produce, among others: `Super effective` (2),
`Not very effective` (3), `No effect` (2), `Critical hit` (2), `Could not move`
(3), `Badly poisoned` (2), `Attack rose` (2), `Harsh sunlight` (2). The census
already reads `Paralysed` and `Badly poisoned` on the battle screen today.

So M4.3's done-when is unreachable against today's vocabulary even after M4.1
cuts the strip to one flag: one flag is frequently three words.

**Options.**

1. **Read the row as "one flag per hit" and amend section 4's wording.** A
   correction to a table, which is what six of Rev 2's seven amendments were. No
   rule moves.
2. **Cut the vocabulary to one word per kind**: `Super`, `Resisted`, `Immune`,
   `Crit`, `Missed`, `Poisoned`. Meets the row as written. Loses `Badly
   poisoned` versus `Poisoned`, which is a real distinction the panel's `TOX`
   chip carries independently.
3. **Go wordless for the kinds that have a glyph.** R8 already ties feedback to
   the forecast's vocabulary, and the forecast is *zero words* — a coloured edge
   and a numeral. The effectiveness flags become the same edge colour plus the
   same fraction on the target, and the strip's word budget is spent on the
   kinds that have no glyph.

**Recommendation: 1, and 3 where M4.2 reaches it.** Option 1 is the honest
reading — D7 was filed to give the strip a row at all, and "1 per hit" plainly
meant one flag. Option 3 is where M4.2 is pointed anyway; it should be built
because it is the right encoding, not to make a number fit.

### D24. The event line is prose at rest on the battle screen

**Blocks M4.3.**

R11: *"The battle screen shows the turn header, the panels, the flags and
nothing written."* Forbids *"a text line for turn order."*

`ui/copy/events.ts` renders `Opposing Snorlax used Body Slam` into
`.flags__event` on every turn. It is V5's, it is deliberate, and its own header
argues for it: a Splash that did nothing worth a flag still says what was used.
It costs one word after proper nouns are excluded (`used`, or `came in for`),
and it is a sentence at rest on the one screen R11 names.

M4.3 says *"if any turn-order text line survives on the battle screen, remove
it"* — which reads as licence to delete it, without saying so.

**Options.**

1. **Delete the line.** R11 as written. The fact "which move was used" is
   re-encoded by the move button that was pressed and by the jiggle that says
   who acted; the sheet has the sentence.
2. **Keep it and amend R11.** Requires a disconfirmer observed in a playtest
   under section 10.1. None has been recorded, so this is not available today —
   it is available to M7.1.
3. **Re-encode it wordless**: actor side mark plus the move's name chip, the
   name being a proper noun and free under the counting rule. Keeps the "what
   was used" fact on the board at zero words.

**Recommendation: 3, with 1 as the fallback.** The line is the only thing on the
battle screen that answers "what just happened" when a turn produced no flag,
and C2 says a decision-relevant fact is re-encoded rather than removed. If 3
cannot be drawn inside section 2's nine families, 1 is correct and the report
says the fact moved to the sheet.

### D25. The sheet opens by a labelled button, not by a pull

**Blocks M4.3.**

M4.3: *"the log sheet is reachable by pull."* Today it is reachable by a click on
`flags.history`, a `<button>` reading `History`, wired at
`ui/screens/battle.ts:121`. There is no pull gesture in `ui/log-sheet.ts`.

Two things follow. The item's clause is a build, not a verification. And
`History` is a word at rest on the battle screen, outside the flag strip's
carve-out, so M4.3's census target cannot be met while the button wears a word.

Section 5's component canon has no row for a history control, and R5 forbids
*"a help button"* as an explanation route — the sheet is not an explanation, so
R5 does not reach it, but a glyph-only control is a mechanism the player must
discover, which is section 7's concern.

**Options.**

1. **Build the pull; keep a glyph handle as the visible affordance.** Both
   routes, one word fewer, and the gesture is the discoverable one because the
   handle is drawn where the sheet will come from.
2. **Keep the tap, swap the word for a glyph**, and record the "pull" clause as a
   deviation in `generation.md`. Cheapest; leaves the item's own text unmet.
3. **Keep the tap and the word**, amend section 4 to allow one word of furniture
   on the battle screen.

**Recommendation: 1**, and section 7 gets an exposure label for the handle on
first arrival, which is the mechanism R7 already provides for exactly this.

### D26. The strip's canon says every chip is the same chip; M4.2 gives it a hue

**Blocks M4.2.**

R8: forecast and feedback *"share a colour family and a glyph family and nothing
else."* M4.2: *"the feedback flag uses the same colour family and glyph family as
the forecast edge on the button."*

`styles.css:545`: *"**Every chip in here is the same chip** … no `--chip`, no
per-kind hue, no size or weight modifier. A super effective flag drawn heavier
than a not-very-effective one would make the strip a recommendation."*
`data/flagWords.ts` says the same from its side: *"There is no size, no colour
and no emphasis field, because … a `weight` column would be the first place that
rule broke."* `test/battle-feedback.test.ts:672` asserts it: *"draws every flag
on one chip recipe, with no per-kind weight or hue."*

The two are not the same claim. The shipped rule forbids a **weight axis** —
one kind drawn louder than another. R8 asks for an **encoding axis** — the same
family on both sides of the same fact. `--stage-up` and `--stage-down` are
already applied to `super` and `resisted` on the button with equal weight, and
the fraction beside the edge is what makes the pair colour-blind safe.

The row is worth filing anyway, because the item cannot land without rewriting a
rule set, a data-file header and a named test, and a session that does that
without a ruling looks exactly like a patch quietly doing something else.

**Options.**

1. **Hue on the effectiveness kinds only** (`super`, `resisted`, `immune`),
   from the forecast's own tokens, plus the `effectiveness-edge` glyph. Every
   other kind stays neutral. The rule becomes "no weight axis", stated as such.
2. **Hue on every kind**, a family per class. Nine families of chip on one line
   is section 7's objection at scale.
3. **No hue; share the glyph family only.** Meets R8 partially and makes the
   forecast and the feedback look unrelated, which is the thing R8 exists to
   prevent.

**Recommendation: 1.** It is the minimum that satisfies R8, it reuses tokens
already on the board, and the equal-weight rule survives in the form that was
always meant: the two directions of one fact are drawn the same size.

## Gates, and the traps this tree has already paid for

- `npm run check` is the gate, ~11 minutes. Run it in the background. **`exit=1`
  with zero failures is normal**: `scripts/check.mjs` classifies the reporter RPC
  timeout as `ERRORED`. Check `grep -c "FAIL "` before believing an exit code.
- `contentHash` is `d4e080` and must not move. `core/` is untouched by Tier 4
  except the mapper, which the record explicitly permits.
- **A test whose premise the design changed gets updated, not weakened.** Three
  will change: `battle-feedback.test.ts:637` "names what the turn did, in the
  protocol's order" and `:672` "no per-kind weight or hue", and the hand-written
  injection block in `test/flags.test.ts` that drives `moveIdentityOf`.
- **The walkers model interactions.** If M4.3 makes the sheet open on a pull,
  `scripts/smoke.mjs` and `scripts/visual/browser.mjs` both need the gesture —
  Tier 2 lost a night to a confirm band neither bot knew about.
- **The census must not be taught to flatter the item.** D17B is the precedent.
  Before M4.3 sets a target, establish what the battle row's current words
  actually are: six of them (`A loaded board`, `A trainer left`) come from the
  gallery fixture's node label and opponent name, which are harness content, not
  app copy. Either the fixture gets proper-noun names or the census says why it
  counts them — decided in the open, not by a selector added quietly.
